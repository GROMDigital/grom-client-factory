The weekly GHL auditor

It reads a sub-account we manage, works out what that account is for, examines every part of it with a separate expert, and produces a ranked list of what is losing money and what to write instead. It is never told what to look at. Everything it knows about the account, it derives from the account.

What it isAn instrument, not a report generator

The product answers six questions every week: why leads do not reply, why they do not engage the AI, why they do not book, why booked leads do not show, why cancellations never rebook, and why nobody progresses. It answers them for one sub-account at a time, read-only, and it never touches the account.

Two things make it different from a checklist. There are no hardcoded best-practice detectors: no rule anywhere fires because a nurture has four emails instead of five. And there is no per-account configuration telling it what anything is for: no file says that 05 No-Show Recovery is the no-show recovery. Real experts read real evidence and are held to a contract about what a finding has to contain.

The one exception is deliberate, and it is explained in the targets section: the account carries the numbers you are aiming at. Those are stated as context, never as a test, and the expert is told to disagree with them if they are wrong.

That second rule is why it works on accounts nobody built to a template. Point it at Francesca, SK Skin or Alevere and it maps whatever is actually there.

The shapeFour stages of expert, with a deterministic command between every one

A model call is the only unpredictable step in the whole product. The audit kernel is the opposite: it checkpoints every phase, canonicalises the output, and byte-compares it if the run resumes. A run that answered differently the second time quarantines itself. So a kernel that called an expert mid-run would quarantine itself on the first resume, every time.

The answer is a seam that repeats: a deterministic command writes the question, the experts answer it as files, and the next deterministic command reads those answers and writes the next question. Hand the same answers back and you get byte-identical results.

Before any of itWhat the evidence actually is

Two rails feed the run. The public rail goes through a governed allowlist and brings back contacts, opportunities, appointments, conversations and calendars: the movement of real people, which becomes the KPI table. The internal rail is a read-only server over the account's own session and brings back what the public API does not expose: the full step graph of every workflow, the runtime of those workflows, the AI agents' instructions, and the email library so a send step that points at a template arrives with its actual words rather than just a subject line.

Nothing is redacted. This is an internal tool reading accounts we administer, and a hole in the evidence is indistinguishable from a hole in the account: a redaction does not read as "we chose not to look", it reads as "there is nothing there".

One limit travels with every single prompt and is repeated in every output: nothing proves the configuration we read today was the configuration in force during the window. A configuration may be called consistent with an outcome, or said to produce one going forward. It may never be said to have caused a past outcome.

The four stagesWho reads what, and why it is split this way

The previous design split each workflow across three analysts and none of them saw it whole. One got triggers, steps and runtime with no copy. One got the messages with no idea that 184 steps had been skipped or that the sequence never stops when a lead replies. Neither could write the sentence that mattered. That is the mistake the four stages fix.

Stage 1
The map
1 expert, always

One systems analyst reads the entire account cold and works out what journey it runs, what each workflow's job actually appears to be, which sit on the money path, which are delivery, and which look abandoned. It classifies but does not diagnose: there is deliberately no field on a map for a problem, because a diagnosis made here would be repeated by every expert downstream and arrive at the ranking stage looking like nineteen independent corroborations of one opinion.

The answer is refused unless it covers every workflow exactly once and names only workflows and KPI edges the account actually has. That check is what makes the map safe to hand to everyone else.

On Grom UK, first attempt, accepted with no repair 27 of 27 workflows mapped. Four whose name disagrees with their behaviour. Eight structural gaps, including the one no configuration file could ever have produced: the Pilot Program is free for 30 days and the client only pays on results, and no workflow in the account is pointed at the trial or at converting a trialing client into a paying one.
Stage 2
One expert per object
19 on Grom UK

A copywriter and systems analyst in one person, handed a single workflow and asked to make it work. Each sees six views of its own object and nothing about any other, because an analyst handed one workflow reads every message in it and the same analyst handed twenty-seven reads none of them properly.

configruntimeevery message in fullits place in the accountthe KPIs it should movethe stage-1 map

The count comes from the account, and the map decides it: a one-message workflow on the money path gets an expert, a one-message receipt does not, and every exclusion is listed with its reason. The output is prose against a rubric, including full replacement copy for anything it would change. Not findings in a schema, because there is no shape in that schema for "this asks permission to make a second ask".

What one expert wrote about 05 No-Show Recovery stopOnResponse is false and nothing removes anybody, so a lead who rebooks on day one still receives "Last follow-up from me" on day 14 and "Closing this one out" on day 21, while 02 Booking Confirmation is confirming the same call to the same person. Configuration, copy and placement in one thought. No single lane could reach it.
Stage 3
Three account-wide experts
3, always

These are the only experts that produce findings. Each reads the map and all nineteen reviews in full, and each rubric tells it plainly: do not repeat that work. One workflow's weak opening is already recorded. What is wanted here is the same weakness in nine sequences.

  • The lead journey and its KPIs. Where the account loses the most people, in absolute numbers rather than worst percentage, and what it costs.
  • The account as one system. What collides with what, the same job done twice in two places, handoffs that drop people, the stage of the journey nothing covers.
  • Every message as one stream. Not sequence by sequence. Total touches on one person across everything live at once, contradictions between sequences, whether the voice is one voice, whether the channel mix matches where conversations actually end.
Stage 4
The investigation
No model at all

Pure code. It validates every finding, groups the survivors into causes by what they anchor to, scores them, and writes the deliverables. Two findings from different experts that point at the same KPI edge or the same workflow become one cause with two independent supports, and corroboration is most of what evidence strength means here.

It merges on discriminating anchors only. Journey stages are near-universal, so grouping on those once collapsed all 24 findings into a single useless blob.

TargetsThe numbers you are aiming at, and why they are not benchmarks

These are two different things and keeping them apart is what stops this becoming a checklist.

A benchmark is what a competent operation in this exact situation achieves. That depends on context no rule can hold: a Meta lead form and a referral are not the same lead, a held deposit and a free consult are not the same booking. The expert supplies that, from its own knowledge, and it must keep doing so.

A target is what the business has decided to aim at. No amount of evidence reveals it. Grom's own show-rate target is 65% while the strategy call is given away free, and 85% if it ever carries a deposit, and nothing in the account says which of those you chose. That was the genuine gap, so targets now live with the account's context and travel to every expert.

Nothing compares them. No code fires a finding because a rate sits under a target. The instruction that travels with the numbers is: state your own benchmark first, before you look at the target, then say where reality sits against both, and if the target is wrong for this situation say so and say why. Every target carries the reasoning behind it precisely so it can be argued with.

Targets are set only where the step's denominator is unambiguous. The published "lead to booked" bands are measured across all leads, while this account measures it across qualified leads, and a target quietly carrying the wrong denominator would be worse than no target at all.

What this immediately surfaced on Grom UK Two of the three targets cannot be measured at all. Booked call attends reads ALL_SUBJECTS_EXCLUDED in every window, because 21 of 64 appointments were never resolved past their own start time. The raw counts say 17 attended against 26 no-shows, roughly a 40% show rate against a 65% target, but the system correctly refuses to publish that as a rate while a third of the appointments sit unclosed. The first fix is record hygiene, not marketing.

The contractWhy a finding can be thrown out

An expert's answer is a claim, not evidence. Every finding must name one of nine mechanism families, anchor to a measured step, state a numeric benchmark and where that standard comes from, and carry three things that make it possible to disagree with:

  • Two competing explanations, each marked material or not, and whether it was ruled out.
  • The evidence against itself.
  • A discriminating test: what to check, what result supports it, what result refutes it.

A finding missing any of those is refused, not ranked low, and the refusal is printed in the report with its reason. An unfalsifiable opinion cannot reach the backlog looking authoritative. One expert fumbling one finding does not cost its other five; one expert returning something that is not a list of findings is fatal, because a missing lane published as a whole investigation is the one error nobody can spot by reading the output.

The outputThree documents and one package per cause

FileWhat it isWho reads it
BACKLOG.md One table, ranked. Cause, confidence, how many experts reached it, impact, effort, risk, score. Effort and risk sit next to impact rather than folded into the score, so you can override the order for a reason the weights cannot know. You, for ten seconds on a Monday
INVESTIGATION.md Every cause in rank order with the full argument: the analysis, the benchmark, the size, the competing explanations nobody ruled out, the evidence against, and the test that would settle it. Anyone who wants to disagree
packages/<causeId>.md One per cause. What is wrong, what to change, where it applies, how you will know it worked, and what to check before touching anything. Whoever implements it
reviews/*.md The nineteen per-object reviews, kept. This is where the replacement copy lives, message by message. Whoever rewrites the sequences

Solution packages are for a human to implement and the file says so on its first line. They are not executable change sets. Making them executable means every touched object bound by id and version, which is a contract no expert is currently asked to produce, and extending it is a decision rather than a task.

Your questionsStraight answers

Does it produce solution packages? Yes

One per ranked cause, written to investigations/<runId>/packages/. The last live run produced fifteen. Each carries the acceptance test that decides whether the fix worked, written by the expert who found the problem rather than invented afterwards.

A prioritised backlog? Yes

BACKLOG.md, ranked by seven criteria with effort and risk counting against. Ordered so the first row is the thing to do this week.

Does it compare this week against last week? Not wired

No, and I would rather say that plainly than let the ledger's existence imply otherwise. There is a built and tested component for it, lib/memory.mjs: an append-only, hash-chained event ledger with a backlog projection that already models a finding opening, being approved, being implemented, being verified, and regressing. It has its own test file.

Nothing calls it. I grepped every file in lib/ and cli/ and the only importer is its own test. So no run has ever written a finding into it, and no run has ever read one back. Week-over-week is a component sitting on the bench, not a feature.

Does it save this week's data so next week can read it? Half

The data persists completely. Every run seals its evidence, its briefs, its map, all nineteen reviews, the investigation and every package on disk under its own run id, and a local database records the run, its checkpoints and its provenance. Nothing is thrown away, and two runs can never overwrite each other: the artefacts are write-once and byte-compared, so re-running the same run id either produces the identical bytes or stops.

What is missing is the join. Nothing turns this week's causes into ledger events, and nothing loads last week's to compare. Next week's run will not know this week happened. Wiring it is a real piece of work, not a switch, and it is the obvious next build after the first live run through the new chain.

Running itThe whole cycle, in order

audit run --mode weekly …
Collects, measures, writes the briefs and the stage-1 question.
→ dispatch 1 agent
Its whole instruction is prompt-account-map.md. Save the JSON it returns.
audit map --map <file>
Validates the map, writes one prompt per object.
→ dispatch N agents, in parallel
One per prompt in reviews/. Each writes to the answer file the command named.
audit reviews
Collects them, records any that never arrived, writes the three lane prompts.
→ dispatch 3 agents, in parallel
One per lane. Each writes a JSON array of findings.
audit investigate --findings <dir>
Refuses, groups, ranks, and writes the backlog, the investigation and the packages.
audit briefs
At any point: which stage this run is at and what to dispatch next, read off the disk rather than a status field.

Briefs and reviews live under private/ and quote real message copy, so they are evidence and never publication material. Nothing in this cycle writes to a GoHighLevel account. The authority for what the product is remains PRODUCT-SPEC.md; the current position of the build is in .superpowers/sdd/STATE.md.
